Outcome

A GRC platform that reads less like a regulatory inventory and more like a guided, AI-assisted workflow. Compliance progress is visible at every level (framework → control → task → evidence), the AI feels woven into the product rather than bolted on, and onboarding gets a non-technical SMB owner from "I don't know where to start" to a configured framework in a structured wizard flow.
Сhallenge

Compliance tools dump dozens of frameworks, hundreds of controls, and stacks of policies on users who already have day jobs. The previous version followed that pattern: users entered company info, the product surfaced what they had to comply with, and left them to figure out the rest.

The redesign reframes the product from a regulatory inventory into a guided workflow. Same core feature, but now the user lands on a clear picture of where they stand, what's next, and why it matters. AI is woven in to surface relevant content and give real-time feedback as users move through the work.

The design question: how do you make a GRC platform feel like a journey toward audit-readiness, not a wall of checklists?
Approach

- Requirements & IA. Translated raw product docs and competitive analysis into six core areas (Onboarding, Dashboard, Frameworks, Policies, Tasks, Settings) - each with a defined user need, primary CTA, and empty-state strategy.
- User flows. Mapped the end-to-end journey: framework selection → policy generation → AI task assignment → evidence collection → post-audit feedback loop. Every screen ties to a progress metric.
- UX system. Cross-product rules to cut cognitive load: one-line context explainer per page, single primary CTA, empty states that teach, and an AI Assistant that surfaces the next action in context - not as a separate feature.
- UI design. Built the full interface in Figma - Green/Yellow/Red triage dashboards, drill-down for control gaps, inline evidence upload on task views, and an onboarding wizard handling the messy setup (existing vs. starter policies, frameworks, team invites).
- AI in UX. Embedded AI as native moments, not a section: framework recommendations at onboarding, "+5% to compliance" task hints, framework gap analysis, policy generation in empty states.
